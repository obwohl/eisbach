# Cleanup audit, September 2026

A survey of the repository ahead of a cleanup pass: what is measurably wrong, what only
looks wrong, and what to do in which order. Findings are evidence-backed; where a claim
could not be verified, that is stated.

Companion document: [`docs/model.md`](model.md) covers the network architecture, its
inert code paths, and four traps for the next training run. This document is about
everything else, and is disposable once acted on.

**Baseline at the time of the audit.** `pytest -q` → 67 passed, 2 skipped.
`ruff check .` → clean. Ruff runs `E,F,W,I,UP,B`, so unused imports and unused locals are
already structurally excluded — that class of finding does not exist here. Sizes: pack
6.80 MiB, `.git` 7.1 M, `data/` 27 M (17 M archive + 11 M checkpoint cache), `archive/`
12 M, `eisbach/` 496 K.

The two skips are `tests/test_model_vendored.py:165,188`; they need the `ts_proba_cuda`
checkout, which no longer exists.

## 1. What the archive proves about the forecast

The archive is now long enough to answer "is this any good?" honestly for the first time.
Method: join on `target_time`, lead = `target_time − reference_time`, CRPS as the
trapezoidal integral of pinball loss over the seven available levels (so it covers
τ ∈ [0.01, 0.99] and mildly *under*states true CRPS). Uncertainty is a **block bootstrap
resampling whole 96-hour runs** — rows within a run are massively autocorrelated, and
row-level intervals would be roughly 10× too narrow.

### Skill, current checkpoint only (59 runs, 5 228 rows, targets 2026-08-04 → 09-04)

| lead | n | MAE | RMSE | bias | CRPS |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0–96 h | 5 228 | **0.411** | 0.530 | −0.018 | 0.289 |
| 0–24 h | 1 409 | 0.324 | 0.424 | −0.006 | 0.225 |
| 24–48 h | 1 347 | 0.428 | 0.531 | −0.050 | 0.291 |
| 48–72 h | 1 274 | 0.472 | 0.593 | −0.019 | 0.324 |
| 72–96 h | 1 198 | 0.431 | 0.569 | +0.005 | 0.323 |

Block-bootstrap 95 % CIs over all leads: MAE 0.411 [0.376, 0.448]; RMSE 0.530
[0.484, 0.577]; bias −0.018 [−0.109, +0.074]; CRPS 0.289 [0.269, 0.310].

### Against persistence (observation at `reference_time` held flat, 11 852 rows)

| lead | persistence MAE | model MAE | skill |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0–24 h | 0.644 | 0.390 | +39 % |
| 24–48 h | 0.850 | 0.581 | +32 % |
| 48–72 h | 1.038 | 0.734 | +29 % |
| 72–96 h | 1.142 | 0.780 | +32 % |
| 0–96 h | 0.914 | 0.617 | **+32 %** |

The model beats persistence at every lead, roughly uniformly. This is an honest result on
live forecasts and is the first defensible skill claim the project has.

### The confound that cannot be removed

Legacy-migrated runs end 2026-08-01; instrumented runs begin 2026-08-04. **They do not
overlap.** Pooled legacy MAE is 0.786 against the current era's 0.411, but era is
perfectly confounded with season — by target month the bias runs June −0.94, July −0.36,
August −0.05, September +0.08. Whether the code got better or June is simply harder cannot
be separated, and no amount of reprocessing will fix it retroactively.

For the same reason the pooled all-eras numbers (MAE 0.627, CRPS 0.424) blend two
different systems and should not be quoted.

### Calibration: under-confident, and *not* correctable yet

Empirical fraction of observations falling below each predicted quantile, current
checkpoint, all leads, n = 5 228 across 59 runs:

| nominal | 1 % | 5 % | 25 % | 50 % | 75 % | 95 % | 99 % |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| empirical | 0.04 % | 1.18 % | 18.6 % | 49.5 % | 81.9 % | 99.0 % | 99.94 % |
| 95 % CI | [0.00, 0.12] | [0.27, 2.44] | [13.6, 23.8] | [42.5, 56.5] | [77.0, 86.6] | [98.0, 99.7] | [99.8, 100] |

PI50 captures 63.3 % [59.0, 67.6] against a nominal 50; PI90 captures 97.8 %
[96.4, 99.0] against 90. Reading it:

- **The median is well calibrated.** 49.5 %, CI straddling 50; bias −0.02 °C, CI
  straddling zero. Nothing to fix in central tendency.
- **The spread is too wide at every level, symmetrically** — 25→18.6 and 75→81.9 mirror
  each other, as do 5→1.2 and 95→99.0. A pure scale problem, no shift and no skew.
- **q0.01 and q0.99 are empirically dead weight.** Only 0.09 % of observations have ever
  fallen outside that band.
- **Under-confidence grows with lead.** Mean absolute error goes 0.32 → 0.43 °C over the
  horizon (×1.33) while width90 goes 2.00 → 3.49 (×1.75).

**But a static correction makes things worse.** Splitting the live archive 60/40 by
reference time and fitting on the early half:

| correction fitted on early half | test pinball | vs. raw 0.1022 |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| per-horizon median bias | 0.1259 | **23 % worse** (MAE 0.415 → 0.543) |
| rolling 7-day bias | 0.1058 | worse |
| rolling 14-day bias | 0.1055 | worse |
| rolling 28-day bias | 0.1049 | worse |

The optimal interval-width factor is **1.03** on the early half and **0.70** on the late
half; cross-applying degrades both. This reconciles the two apparently conflicting
readings: the "optimal 0.71" measured on the current checkpoint *is* the late half. The
cold bias is a **regime effect** — the model lags the water during the June/July warming
and loses it once the season plateaus — not a fixable offset.

**Conclusion: do not deploy post-hoc calibration.** Revisit after a full seasonal cycle,
and fit conditionally (on season, or on the sign of the recent water trend) rather than on
horizon alone.

### Archive inventory and damage

17 MB, 14 files, all git-tracked, month-partitioned CSV. No truncation; every file ends
with a complete row.

- **`forecasts/`** — 9.4 MB, 23 616 rows, 246 distinct reference times, **exactly 96 rows
  each**, leads 1–96 with no exception. Zero NaNs in `wassertemp_q*`, zero non-monotone
  rows. Schema stable across all five partitions.
- **`observations/`** — 52 KB, 1 714 rows, 2026-06-24 → 2026-09-04. **Zero gaps**: 1 714
  expected hourly slots, 1 714 present. The cleanest part of the archive. Hard constraint:
  observations begin 53 days *after* the first archived forecast, so 43 live runs
  (May–June) can never be scored.
- **`weather/`** — 6.9 MB, **two incompatible schemas**. May–July is the raw Bright Sky
  payload (20 columns, no `reference_time`); August onward is the normalized 6-column
  frame. The cutover is 2026-08-04 10:00Z, which is also when `replay` backtests first
  appear — adding `reference_time` is exactly what `load_weather_snapshot` needs.

**Confirmed data loss.** Seven of the thirteen pre-August weather snapshots have a
**completely empty `timestamp` column** — 840 of 1 560 rows:

```
2026-06: 06-06T10:02:52, 06-18T22:03:10, 06-23T22:03:14
2026-07: 07-08T22:03:22, 07-13T22:03:31, 07-18T22:02:52, 07-23T22:03:05
```

The values are there; the index is gone. These cannot be replayed, and since historical
DWD forecasts are a paid product, they are unrecoverable. Nothing in the repo records
this today.

### Provenance gaps

- **`model_id` is blank on 46 % of rows** — every legacy-migrated row, because
  `migrate_legacy_forecasts` hardcodes `model_id=""`. Whether the checkpoint ever changed
  before 2026-07-24 is **unanswerable**. Only one non-empty value exists,
  `1c7a531768d8`.
- **1 824 rows have a blank `issued_at`** — 14 oracle runs in July, 5 live runs in August.
  `_resolve_precedence` tie-breaks on `issued_at` with `na_position="first"`, so these
  rows would silently *lose* a same-kind tie.
- **One non-causal row**: reference 2026-08-31 22:00Z, target 23:00Z, issued 23:37:03Z —
  a 1-hour lead "forecast" 37 minutes after the fact. Median issue lag is 0.39 h (max
  1.62 h), so this is a one-off from a late run, not systemic. Impact: 1 row in 12 284.
- **The precedence rule has never fired.** Zero reference times carry more than one kind.
  This is structural, not luck: all 67 backtest anchors sit at exactly −96/−192/−288 h
  from live runs that happen at irregular clock hours, so anchors essentially never
  collide. The machinery in `_resolve_precedence` is correct but has never been exercised
  in production.
- **Cadence is not thrice-daily in the data.** The legacy era ran twice daily at 10:00 and
  22:00; the current era's anchors scatter across 16 different hours, because
  `reference_time` tracks the last gauge reading rather than the cron. Three real outages
  are visible: 2026-05-09 → 06-01 (**23 days**), 2026-06-10 → 06-18 (195 h), and
  2026-05-07 → 05-09 (48 h).

## 2. Growth: the bloat is coming back, slower

Measured on `data/archive/weather/2026-09.csv`: 9 snapshots × exactly 1 152 rows (48 days
hourly = `HISTORY_DAYS` 40 + `FORECAST_DAYS` 8). Only **1 728 of 10 368 rows (16.7 %)**
fall strictly after their own `reference_time` — and those are the only rows
`_replay_weather` ever reads (`inference.py:118-121` takes everything at or before the
anchor from the live frame, and only `index > reference_time` from the snapshot). The
other 83 % is 40 days of already-past weather, re-archived three times a day.

| | per run | per year | 5 years |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| weather partition | 114 KiB | 122 MiB | 610 MiB |
| forecasts partition | 48 KiB | 51 MiB | 255 MiB |
| observations | ~0.1 KiB | 0.3 MiB | 1.5 MiB |
| **working tree** | | **~173 MiB/yr** | **~865 MiB** |
| git pack delta | 25.6 KiB/commit | ~27 MiB/yr | ~137 MiB |

A fresh clone lands near **1 GB after five years** — at GitHub's recommended-limit
boundary, and slow long before that.

Separately, `airtemp_96_q*` and `pressure_96_q*` are roughly **two thirds of the 9.4 MB
forecast store** and are, as far as anyone can find, never read back.

**Compression is the wrong answer.** gzip or monthly parquet would halve the working tree
but turn every rewrite into a fresh incompressible blob: pack cost would jump from
~25 KiB/commit to the full file size (~1 MB and rising), which is strictly worse overall,
and it discards the hand-readability that `archive.py:27-31` explicitly argues for.
Row-trimming is the only measure that improves both numbers at once.

## 3. Code and infrastructure

### High

**`eisbach/validate.py` has zero test coverage.** `grep -rn validate tests/` returns
nothing. This is the module that decides whether a run publishes or fails, and it holds
real logic: `_check_ranges` (`:38`), the truncation-leak check (`:76-81`), and `_coverage`
(`:51-59`) behind the `MIN_COVERAGE = 0.80` gate that hard-fails a run. It is the only
untested production module and the one with the largest blast radius. Pure addition, no
risk.

### Medium

**The `.tmp` gitignore gap.** `_write_partition` (`archive.py:128-133`) writes
`<partition>.csv.tmp` then `os.replace`s it. `.gitignore:18` un-ignores everything under
`data/archive/`, so a `.tmp` file is **not ignored** — confirmed with
`git check-ignore -v`. `forecast.yml:67` does `git add data/archive`. An interrupted run
leaves a `.tmp` behind and the next run commits it into the irreplaceable archive. One
line to fix. `public/` should be added too.

**Unpinned dependencies in an unattended pipeline.** `forecast.yml:48` runs
`pip install -e .` against `pandas>=2.2`, `matplotlib>=3.8`, `torch>=2.3`, `scipy>=1.10`
with no lock. A breaking upstream release takes the forecast down at 04:00 UTC, and CI
resolves independently at a different time — so CI can be green while the forecast run is
broken.

**The archive push does not rebase.** `forecast.yml:63-70` pushes without
`git pull --rebase`. The `concurrency: forecast` group serialises forecast runs against
each other, so only a human push to `main` inside the ~5-minute window triggers this — but
when it does, the run fails and `Report failure` opens an issue for a transient
non-fast-forward.

**`Report failure` opens unbounded issues.** `forecast.yml:85-97` creates a new issue on
every failure with no search for an existing open `forecast-failure` issue. A persistent
breakage — GKD changing its table markup, say — produces 3 issues/day indefinitely.

**The backtest-honesty explanation is written out in full in six places**: `CLAUDE.md:36-42`,
`README.md:40-53`, `archive.py:6-25`, `inference.py:6-22`, `plotting.py:12-17`,
`docs/page/index.html:28-32`. All six were checked against the code and **all six are
currently correct**. So this is a maintenance hazard, not a present bug: the project's
central invariant has six copies that must move together. One imprecision worth fixing:
`README.md:51` says `live` is "retrieved rather than recomputed", but
`_resolve_backtest` step 1 accepts `kinds=[KIND_LIVE, KIND_REPLAY]` — a stored *replay* is
reused for free too.

### Low

**`plotting.py:19-20` gives a factually wrong reason for a correct decision.** It claims
importing `inference` at module scope would close a cycle. There is no cycle: `inference`
imports `archive`, `data` and `model`, none of which import `plotting`. The `TYPE_CHECKING`
guard is the right pattern and there *is* a real reason to keep it — importing `inference`
would drag `torch` into `plotting`'s import graph via `eisbach.model` — but that reason is
not the one written down.

**Three separate implementations of "naive means UTC, then convert to Europe/Berlin"**:
`plotting.py:89-106`, `plotting.py:218-223`, `inference.py:259-265`, plus
`archive._as_utc` doing the UTC half. `LOCAL_TIMEZONE` is defined once in `data.py:17` and
then hardcoded as a string literal at `inference.py:264` and `plotting.py:105,106,223`.

**`plotting.py:235` round-trips the colour list through the cycler it built from it** —
`PRIMER_STYLE['axes.prop_cycle'].by_key()['color']` to recover `PLOT_COLORS`.

**`migrate_legacy_forecasts` (`archive.py:430-470`) is a shim with no production caller.**
41 lines, called only from four tests. Its target,
`data/forecast_archive/water_temp_predictions_archive.csv`, is gitignored, absent from the
working tree, and **never appears anywhere in git history**. The archive already holds May
2026 data, so the migration has clearly been run. Confirm no untracked local copy exists
before deleting.

**`archive.read_forecasts` is test-only but should stay** — it is a legitimate public read
API for anyone analysing the archive by hand. Noted so a future pass does not flag it as
dead.

**`ci.yml` and `forecast.yml` duplicate their setup block**, and `ci.yml` has no
`paths-ignore`. The two workflows do not currently overlap: the bot's archive commits use
the default `GITHUB_TOKEN`, and GitHub suppresses workflow triggering for those. That guard
is implicit — swap in a PAT or deploy key (which `docs/scheduling.md:29-49` already
contemplates) and CI starts running the full torch install three times a day on commits
that touch nothing but CSVs. Also, `ci.yml` tests only Python 3.12 while
`pyproject.toml:12` claims `>=3.11`. Whether ruff and pytest actually gate merges depends
on branch-protection settings, which are not visible from the checkout.

**`forecast.yml:22-26` grants workflow-wide permissions the `publish` job does not need.**

**Dependencies check out.** All of `pandas`, `requests`, `beautifulsoup4`, `matplotlib`,
`scipy`, `torch` are directly imported; `einops` by the vendored layers. Nothing is
imported-but-undeclared. `cycler` *is* genuinely imported (`plotting.py:31`), so the
declaration is correct — though it is matplotlib's own dependency and `plt.cycler`
re-exports it, so the declaration could be dropped. `numpy` is declared but never imported
outside the vendored tree and the tests; it is a transitive dependency of everything else
and `PROVENANCE.md:132` lists it deliberately — leave it.

### The `archive/` directory

12 MB working tree, but only **2.66 MiB packed**. 29 files: three 10-year CSVs (9.8 MB
together), twelve PNGs (~1.5 MB), six scripts, two READMEs, four small result CSVs.
Nothing references it — grep for `isar_eisbach_comparison` and `autogluon_chronos_bolt`
outside `archive/` returns zero hits across `.py`, `.md`, `.yml`, `.toml`, `.html`. It is
excluded from ruff and pytest; running ruff on it anyway reports 124 errors, so the
exclusion is correct.

`archive/README.md` is genuinely good — it documents three real traps, and its central
claim was verified: the 0.450/0.311 vs 0.624/0.420 figures quoted in the top-level README
are exactly `finetuned_showdown.csv`, and the README explains why that file is quoted
despite its misleading name.

**The naming collision is the actual problem.** Three things named "archive": `archive/`
(disposable research), `data/archive/` (irreplaceable), `eisbach/archive.py` (the module).
No code confuses them. The realistic hazard is a human or an agent typing `rm -rf archive/`
meaning the research junk and hitting the wrong one — or reading CLAUDE.md's "`data/archive/`
is irreplaceable" and generalising it. That is one keystroke between "frees 12 MB" and
"destroys the only copy of every forecast ever made".

| Option | Cost | Buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep as-is | 0 | Nothing. The README warning is the only mitigation. |
| **Rename to `research/`** | one `git mv` + 4 references | Kills the collision. History follows the rename. |
| Tag and delete | a tag + `git rm -r` | Frees 12 MB from the working tree and **0 bytes from the pack** — the tag keeps the blobs reachable. Also trades a browsable record for a `git show` incantation, and `weather_10_years_pressure.csv` has no generator anywhere. |
| Split to a second repo | highest | Same as above, plus coordination. |

Renaming is recommended: the size argument for deleting is weak (2.66 MiB packed, 22 % of
one month of `data/archive`), and the collision is the real damage.

## 4. Proposed order of work

### A — Safety net (pure addition, no risk)

1. `tests/test_validate.py`: NaN in a quantile column; a value outside
   `PLAUSIBLE_RANGES`; a forecast index overlapping observations (the leak check);
   coverage below and above `MIN_COVERAGE`; `_coverage` returning `None` on no overlap
   (should warn and continue, not raise).
2. Add `data/archive/**/*.tmp` and `public/` to `.gitignore`. **Done.**

### B — Documentation and naming (zero risk, one commit)

3. Fix "twice a day" in `CLAUDE.md:50` and `validate.py:5`; the `outputs`-branch comment
   in `.gitignore`; the 900 KB figure in `archive.py:29`; the false import-cycle claim in
   `plotting.py:19-20`; `README.md:69`'s dev-extras instruction; the `live`/`replay`
   imprecision at `README.md:51`; CLAUDE.md's layout block. **Done**, except the 900 KB
   figure in `archive.py:29`.
4. Point the three module docstrings at `archive.py`'s as the canonical statement of the
   precedence rule instead of restating it, leaving CLAUDE.md and README as the two
   audience-facing copies.
5. `git mv archive/ research/`, updating `pyproject.toml:53`, `.gitattributes:7`,
   `CLAUDE.md:16`, `README.md:85`. **Done** — also the six self-references inside
   `research/README.md`.
6. Record the seven timestamp-less weather snapshots somewhere durable — they are
   invisible today. **Done** — CLAUDE.md, under "Things that will bite you".

### C — Growth and provenance (changes write behaviour; do A first)

7. Trim weather snapshots in `write_weather_snapshot` to rows after
   `reference_time` minus a small splice margin. Cuts the weather store ~6× and total
   growth from ~173 to ~71 MiB/yr. **Only future writes** — existing partitions stay
   untouched; do not retro-compact irreplaceable data for 15 MB.
8. Add observed `lufttemperatur_c` and `pressure` to the `observations` store. This
   resolves the tension created by item 7 (which drops archived observed weather) and is
   the prerequisite for decomposing error into "the model was wrong about the river"
   versus "DWD was wrong about the air". A few KB per month.
9. Make `issued_at` non-nullable at write time, and give legacy rows a placeholder
   `model_id` such as `unknown-pre-20260724` rather than the empty string, which reads as
   "no model".
10. Add a `schema_version` column to the weather store so the two datasets sharing that
    directory are self-describing.
11. Optionally: stop writing `airtemp_96_q*` / `pressure_96_q*`, or move them to their own
    store. Two thirds of the forecast store, never read.

### D — Groundwork for the model

12. **Persist a verification table.** Everything in section 1 was recomputed from scratch;
    nothing accumulates. Storing per-run MAE/CRPS/PIT/coverage by lead as observations
    close gives a trend line — "is the model drifting?" — which is invisible today, and it
    is the prerequisite for ever fitting a calibration. `validate.py:35` already computes
    an interval coverage; extending and persisting it is the cheap version.
13. **Re-archive the richer DWD fields.** The pre-August payload carried `sunshine`,
    `solar`, `cloud_cover`, `relative_humidity`, `wind_speed`, `dew_point` and
    `precipitation`; the August schema change kept only temperature, precipitation and
    pressure. Solar radiation is the physically obvious driver of a shallow urban channel
    that air temperature alone cannot explain, and pressure — the one covariate the mask
    lets water attend to — is measurably inert (see `docs/model.md`). Costs almost nothing
    now, and covariates not archived are gone.
14. **Record input-completeness per run**: missing gauge hours, anchor age against wall
    clock, weather rows fetched, hours interpolated. The one non-causal row and the 1.6 h
    maximum issue lag suggest runs do sometimes limp, and there is currently no way to
    exclude a degraded run from verification.
15. **Do not ship post-hoc calibration.** See section 1 — every variant tried was worse out
    of sample. Revisit after a full seasonal cycle.

The real model lever — reconnecting the covariate pathway — needs a retraining run and is
documented in [`docs/model.md`](model.md) rather than here. The short version: the mask
never lets air temperature reach the water channel, RevIN discards the absolute level of
every covariate, and pressure changes the forecast by at most 0.018 °C. The checkpoint
already carries a `channel_adjacency_prior` whose first row says "water may see
everything", with the feature switched off.

### Not to be touched

`eisbach/model/duet/` stays byte-identical to upstream. The four bugs documented in
`docs/model.md` are notes for the next training run, not patches for this repository.

`pytest -q` and `ruff check .`, then `python main.py` twice against a scratch
`archive_root`, confirming the second run's −96 h backtest resolves as `live` or `replay`
and not `oracle`. That single assertion exercises the archive write path, the precedence
